01 / DATA & PRIVACY

Privacy Policy

This page describes the intended privacy approach for the Pravah International website. It is a general template, not a representation that a production data-processing programme or certification is already in place.

Information submitted

The pilot enquiry interface currently runs entirely in the visitor's browser. It validates fields and creates a local preview receipt. In this supplied demo code, form details are not transmitted to Pravah International, saved to a server, or sent to a third party. Do not enter real identity documents, payment details or confidential information into the demo.

Production deployment and purposes

If a production enquiry form is connected, Pravah should identify the legal entity acting as controller, the purposes and lawful bases for processing, the categories of data collected, recipients/processors, retention periods, international transfer safeguards, and a contact route for privacy requests before collection begins.

Cookies and local storage

The demo stores a cookie-preference choice in the browser's localStorage under the key pravah-cookie-choice-v1. It does not load advertising or analytics trackers. The banner's “Accept” and “Essential Only” actions both dismiss the banner; because optional trackers are not installed, the two choices do not enable different tracking behaviour.

Security and retention

Production systems should use data minimisation, role-based access, encryption in transit and at rest, documented retention and deletion schedules, processor agreements, access logging, incident response procedures and tested backups. Document images and payment credentials should be handled only by appropriately vetted specialist providers, not collected in a general-purpose chat or website form.

Data subject rights

Where applicable law grants rights of access, correction, deletion, restriction, objection, portability or withdrawal of consent, a production privacy notice must explain how to exercise them and identify the responsible entity and contact details. Those details must be completed before launch.

Implementation note: the supplied meta CSP is a defence-in-depth measure, not a substitute for server response headers. Configure the hosting platform to send a strict Content-Security-Policy, X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff, Referrer-Policy and appropriate Permissions-Policy headers.

03 / INTERNATIONAL CLIENTS

Cross-Border B2B Technology Consultancy Clause

For services supplied to a business customer established outside India, the parties should document the customer's legal name, registered address, tax registration details where relevant, place of supply, service description, invoicing currency, payment terms and responsibility for applicable indirect taxes in the signed agreement.

Reverse-charge and indirect tax treatment

Where a transaction qualifies as an export of services or another cross-border B2B supply under the applicable law, the supplier may issue an invoice using the tax treatment permitted by that law, and the business customer may be required to account for VAT, GST or similar tax under a reverse-charge mechanism in its jurisdiction. Reverse charge is not automatic for every international transaction. It depends on the supplier's and customer's status, place-of-supply rules, service classification, establishment and local legislation.

India-side compliance

Where Pravah International contracts and invoices from India, the supplier must independently determine whether the service meets the statutory export-of-services conditions, whether GST registration and a Letter of Undertaking or other export procedure is required, how foreign-exchange realisation must be evidenced, and what invoice, return and recordkeeping obligations apply. A foreign customer address alone does not establish zero-rated export treatment.

Customer cooperation

The customer agrees to provide accurate business and tax information reasonably required to support the agreed tax treatment. Each party remains responsible for taxes imposed on it by law. Any withholding tax, gross-up, VAT registration, local filing or tax recovery arrangement must be expressly addressed in the commercial contract and reviewed by qualified advisers.

This clause is a drafting starting point only. Obtain advice from an Indian GST professional and a tax adviser in the customer's country before issuing cross-border invoices or describing a supply as zero-rated, VAT-exempt or reverse-charge eligible.

04 / DEPLOYMENT CONTROLS

Security & deployment notes

  • Serve the site over HTTPS and configure security headers at the hosting/server layer.
  • Use a backend with server-side validation, rate limiting, CSRF protection, spam controls and secure logging before enabling real enquiry submissions.
  • Never store passport images, payment-card data, secrets or access tokens in browser localStorage.
  • Use payment-provider hosted checkout or tokenisation; do not claim that a pre-authorisation has occurred until the provider confirms it.
  • Use vetted identity-document providers and define human review for failed, ambiguous or exceptional checks.
